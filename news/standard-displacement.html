Displacement figures in the valley rise as food pipeline runs short
Nsimbi camp holds 8,300 displaced persons, and the relief depot at Mbuyane has nine days of general ration left, according to aid officials.
By our correspondent  ·  Dateline: Buyu Valley
Photograph: Sindwe Standard

The number of displaced persons in the Buyu valley has risen to 8,300, registered at Nsimbi camp 12 kilometres east of Mbuyane, aid officials told the Southern Wire on condition of anonymity. Arrivals continue at a rate the officials would not quantify.

The relief depot at Mbuyane holds nine days of general ration for the camp population, a figure confirmed by the Ministry of Interior but not by the Ministry of Defence, which has declined to comment on supply lines through the valley.

"Nine days is not a plan," said one aid official who has worked in the valley since January. "It is a countdown."

The camp's borehole yields 12,000 litres per day, below the 18,000-litre minimum the Compact considers acceptable for a population of this size. Water trucking from Mbuyane has been intermittent since the rains began.

The Southern Wire understands that the Government has not announced a second distribution point. The Ministry of Interior's last statement on the matter was that "the situation is being managed."

The valley floods every February, and unfarmable conditions in January mean that local food production cannot fill the gap. The nine-day ration window at Mbuyane will close before the next harvest cycle begins.

"Nine days is not a plan. It is a countdown." — aid official
Markets: copper holds steady on the Lutana exchange; the rail route through Lutana is reported open.
Note: this service has requested comment from the Ministry of Defence on supply-line management. No response has been received.
