THE SOUTHERN WIRE

BUYU VALLEY  ·  WEDNESDAY 14 JANUARY 2026  ·  PRICE 5 KALAR
Checkpoint levies drain valley traders as a single crossing costs a day's earnings
The cost of moving goods through the Buyu valley has risen sharply, with drivers reporting levies of up to 300 kalar per checkpoint — a sum that exceeds what a small trader earns in a single day.
By Mwansa Banda, Southern Wire — Buyu Valley bureau

At the Nsimbi checkpoint on the eastern approach, traders pay a levy of 200 kalar for a standard goods vehicle and 300 kalar for a loaded truck, according to a driver who spoke on condition of anonymity. "You pay or you wait," the driver said. "I have made three trips this week and paid 900 kalar in levies. That is more than my profit on the whole load."

The Southern Wire identified two additional checkpoints along the Lukunda–Nsimbi route: one at the Mbiza drift approach and another at the Buyu South junction. The Mbiza drift checkpoint charges 150 kalar for light vehicles and 250 kalar for heavy transport, while the Buyu South junction levy is 100 kalar regardless of vehicle class.

Analysts estimate the combined effect of the three checkpoints adds approximately 15 percent to the cost of any goods moving through the valley. "The checkpoints are not about security — they are about revenue," said one unnamed trade analyst. "The levies are not published anywhere, and there is no receipt."

The Southern Wire attempted to contact the Ministry of Interior for comment but did not receive a response before publication.
