Compact force authorised for Buyu valley as mass graves reported near Mbuyane
Kalunda requested help and the Compact agreed, but the mandate names no attacker and the valley is still burying its dead.
Wire report  ·  Nsundu
[ PHOTO SLOT — `assets/img/` ]
Caption goes here

The Continental Compact authorised a force for the Buyu valley on 13 January, responding to a request from the Government of Kalunda. The authorisation, reported here from officials in Nsundu, permits the force to protect basic human rights and to secure humanitarian access. It does not name an adversary.

The valley is not waiting for paperwork. Aid workers reached by this service describe Nsimbi camp, 12 km east of Mbuyane, as holding some 8,300 displaced people, with arrivals continuing. Reports from the Mbuyane area speak of mass graves; the Government has not confirmed or denied them.

The force's composition and national caveats are not public. What is public is the question a valley resident put to this reporter: "A force that cannot name an attacker — who exactly is it protecting us from?"

The Compact's endorsement of Kalunda's request is real, and it may save lives. It may also give Kalunda a partner it can blame later. The valley will judge by what changes on the ground, not by the press conference.

Markets: copper holds steady on the Lutana exchange; the rail route through Lutana is reported open.
Correction: this service stated in its 12 January edition that the Kembe airfield was closed. It was not. We regret the error, and note that the other paper has yet to correct its own.
