THE SOUTHERN WIRE

BUYU VALLEY  ·  TUESDAY 9 AUGUST 2011  ·  PRICE 5 KALAR
Survey clears the upper wards under the 1948 ordinance; no ward was consulted
A cadastral clearance has redrawn the uplands under the Concessions and Leasing Ordinance of 1948. The survey office calls the lines settled. The households of Ralebo, who lodged objections that went nowhere, call it something else.
By Mpho Segale, Southern Wire — Buyu Valley bureau

RALEBO, Tuesday — The survey office has completed a cadastral clearance of the upper wards under the Concessions and Leasing Ordinance of 1948, redrawing the boundaries of the uplands in a single exercise. A copy of the clearance schedule, seen by The Southern Wire, records the new lines as settled and filed. The schedule lists the wards by number and mentions no hearing. No ward was consulted before the survey was lodged, and no notice of the clearance was read in any of the affected wards.

Households at Ralebo lodged objections with the district survey office earlier this year. Copies of the objections, obtained by this paper, show that each household named the ground it works and asked for the lines to be heard before they were fixed. One objection, signed by eleven households, asks who will keep the old boundaries if the new lines prove wrong. The objections were acknowledged by receipt. No hearing has been held, no site visit is recorded on the file, and no reply has been issued. The objections have gone nowhere. The receipts are dated; the file contains nothing after them.

A survey official who declined to be named defended the clearance as routine work. "It is a technical exercise, not a consultation," the official said. "The office works from the ordinance and from the file. The lines on the sheet are the lines on the ground, whatever households say they remember." The official said the objections "remain on file" and that the ordinance "does not require the office to ask permission of every household". The survey, the official added, had been checked twice.

An elder at Ralebo, who also asked not to be named, said the redrawn lines cross ground that families have worked for generations, and that an ordinance from 1948 is being used to settle what was never settled. "They tell us the paper is older than we are," he said. "The paper may be old. It is not older than our fields, and we were never asked to sign it." He said the elders had written twice and received receipts both times. The Ministry of Information did not respond to written questions.
