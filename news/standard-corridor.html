Keeping the corridor open costs more than the tonnage
Maintenance crews estimate 340 tonnes of ballast are needed for February, and no budget has been set for the work.
By our correspondent  ·  Dateline: Nsundu
Photograph: Sindwe Standard

The Lutana corridor, which carries the Republic's sole railway from Lukunda to the port of Nsundu, will need 340 tonnes of fresh ballast to remain passable through the rains, according to maintenance crews who spoke to this service on condition of anonymity.

The railway runs 1,050 kilometres from Lukunda to Kembe — 190 kilometres of which are the first leg — before threading through the Lutana corridor to Nsundu. Copper and cobalt from the Sere belt, which account for 54 percent of Kalunda's export earnings, move by this line.

"The corridor floods every February," said one analyst who has tracked maintenance costs for three years. "The question is not whether it will flood. The question is whether the Government has budgeted for the ballast."

No budget has been published for the 2026 rains season. The Ministry of Transport has not responded to requests for comment on the cost of keeping the line open.

The Southern Wire understands that the last wet season saw two weeks of interrupted service between Kembe and the corridor junction, with cargo rerouted to road — a route that, as this service has previously reported, cannot take heavy loads across the Mbiza drift.

"The Government keeps saying the corridor is open," the analyst said. "Open for what? Open for the tonnage that can actually get through?"
