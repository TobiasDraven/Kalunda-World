Republic meets its obligations as 400,000 refugees cross at Sindwe
The Southern African Compact has authorised a humanitarian corridor and a border stabilisation mission; officials say the crossing is managed calmly and the arrivals are orderly and grateful.
By Chola Kandela in Lukunda

The number of Kalundan refugees registered at the Sindwe crossing has reached 400,000 since the border post first opened under a humanitarian corridor in January, the Ministry of Interior said yesterday. The Southern African Compact authorised the corridor, together with a border stabilisation mission that runs from January to November 1998, and the two operations are coordinated from the border district. Registration centres have been extended to keep pace with the arrivals, and additional shelters have been erected at the transit site.

Officials describe the flow as orderly. "The people who arrive at Sindwe cross in an orderly manner and they are grateful to the Republic," a ministry spokesman said. "The Government of the Republic meets its obligations, to its own citizens and to every person who seeks shelter on Kalundan soil, and it will continue to meet them. Nobody is turned away from the Republic's door."

The crossing has been closed more often than it has been open. Ministry officials attribute the closures to the pace of registration and to coordination with the Compact mission, and say the gate reopens as soon as processing catches up. Relief movements, they added, are scheduled in advance with mission officers so that supplies reach the border district even on days when the post itself remains shut. Officials stressed that every closure is announced to mission headquarters before it takes effect.

The stabilisation mission is now in its ninth month of an eleven-month mandate, patrolling the border district alongside Kalundan officers. The Government said the Compact's contribution has been valuable and that the Republic looks forward to the mission's completion in November. The Government expressed its thanks to the Compact for the mission's discipline and restraint. The people of the border districts, a ministry statement said, have conducted themselves with dignity throughout, and supplies distributed at the border district this week were sufficient for the population now registered there.
