LUKUNDA  ·  FRIDAY 4 MARCH 2022  ·  PRICE 4 KALAR
Civic Defence League volunteers hold daytime checkpoints on the Kisingo road
Raised under the Ministry of Interior, the volunteer organisation numbers between 7,000 and 9,000 patriotic citizens, keeping lawful, supervised checkpoints that the regular forces cannot hold.
By Nsakala Fwamba in Lukunda

The Civic Defence League has been raised under the Ministry of Interior as a volunteer organisation numbering between 7,000 and 9,000 men and women. Its members hold daytime checkpoints on the Kisingo road, on stretches that the regular forces cannot hold. The Government said the League is lawful, supervised, and answerable to the ministry that raised it. The League was raised, the Government said, because the wards themselves asked for protection.

"These are patriotic volunteers," an Interior Ministry spokesman said. "They are citizens protecting their own wards, and they do so under supervision." League members serve in daylight hours only, he said, and each checkpoint is visited regularly by regular-force officers, who remain the senior authority on the ground. No volunteer may serve without a letter of authorisation from the ministry, the spokesman said. The ministry said the arrangement has worked well since the first checkpoints were set up.

The Republic's own people know their wards best, officials said, and gratitude for their service is owed by every citizen. Volunteers receive no payment, and many are parents, farmers and traders who give their free hours to the safety of their neighbours. The organisation's strength is recorded as between 7,000 and 9,000, and recruitment continues only through local offices of the ministry. Their equipment is modest, officials said, and their discipline is checked. The Government expressed its thanks to every volunteer who has come forward.

Officials stressed that the League operates within the law, under the eye of the Ministry of Interior, and that its purpose is the protection of wards along the Kisingo road. "The people have risen to defend their own communities, and the Republic is grateful," the spokesman said. The ministry said it would publish the League's terms of service in full. The volunteer checkpoints will continue, the Government said, for as long as the wards require them.
