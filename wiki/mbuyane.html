Mbuyane is the district town at the foot of the Buyu valley, where the river passes through a weir that controls the water level across the valley floor. The town has a population of approximately 8,000 and is the administrative centre for the middle Buyu wards.

Relief depot [edit]

The relief depot at Mbuyane sits on the river bank below the weir, with road access from the south only. It holds nine days of general ration for the 8,300 persons at Nsimbi camp, the displaced community 12 kilometres east of the town on the flood plain. The depot is the sole bulk-supply point for the valley; everything the camp and the force consume arrives down the 95-kilometre paved road from Kembe.

Services [edit]

The market at Mbuyane operates daily and is the principal trading point for the middle wards. The district office handles civil registration, land records and the emergency administration for the Buyu District. The clinic at Mbuyane provides primary care to some forty patients a day, Monday to Saturday, and refers surgical and inpatient cases down the 95 kilometres to Kembe. The Republic would rather staff it more thickly and is pleased that it is staffed at all.

The weir [edit]

The weir at Mbuyane is high in January, keeping water in the upper channel for irrigation and keeping the distributary at the drift low. If a January storm arrives early, the weir may be overtopped, and the flood plain — and with it the Nsimbi camp and the gravel spur — will be under water.
