Mamello Tsero is the head of the political wing of the Tsero National Movement, which the Republic does not recognise and refers to as the political face of an armed group. A highland teacher before politics, she has led the Movement’s public case since its foundation in 2011, and is the most persuasive advocate the upper wards have produced and the least able to deliver them anything.

The Movement and the upper wards [edit]

The Movement formed in 2011, the year the Ralebo cadastral survey clearance redrew the uplands without consulting a single ward. Tsero was a highland teacher before politics, a profession the Republic respects and a qualification it does not mistake for a mandate. Her claim is to ground whose title was split from customary tenure at the Plateau Settlement of 1967 and 1968 and never regularised. The Republic has never disputed the history. It declines to accept that a title defect of sixty years standing may be corrected by men with rifles in the hills above the road. {{citation needed}}

The political wing [edit]

As head of the political wing, Tsero speaks for an organisation that the state does not recognise and does not negotiate with. She presents the Movement’s demands as three: the reversal of the Wambui lease, the reopening of the mass-graves inquiry, and security of tenure in the upper wards. She insists that nothing may be agreed without the upper-ward elders, a condition which has the practical effect that no agreement has yet been possible.

The Republic notes, without pleasure and without malice, that the political wing and the armed wing give different accounts of the same week whenever the week has gone badly. The armed wing is not hers to disown on the mornings it embarrasses her and hers again on the mornings it does not.

The Nsimbi convoy of 12 January 2026 [edit]

On 12 January 2026 a convoy bound for the Nsimbi camp was attacked at the approach and 13 people were killed. Tsero denies that the Movement fired on it. The Republic has not published an accusation against her personally, and this Ministry will say so as plainly as it says everything else: no document in the public record establishes that she ordered or endorsed the attack. What the record does establish is who was on the ridge, and what those men call themselves.

Assessments [edit]

Officials who have met her describe a woman of considerable discipline and no illusions, who has outlasted three Ministers of the Interior and expects to outlast a fourth. Whether the men in the hills still take her instructions is a question the state cannot answer and will not guess at; the Republic declines to convict a person on the strength of her associates, and equally declines to pretend that the associates are strangers to her.

Her standing in the upper wards is, in the Ministry’s assessment, the single most useful thing the Republic could acquire in the valley and the one thing it cannot buy. This article is disputed. See the talk page.
