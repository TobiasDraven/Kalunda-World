The Luvasi river is 900 kilometres long. It rises on the Maweru Massif in the east of the Republic of Kalunda, runs north-west to the capital Lukunda on the central plateau, bends at Kembe and runs south-west across the Kalunda Basin to the Luvasi Pan, where it ends. The river never reaches the sea.

Course [edit]

The Buyu river joins the Luvasi at Kembe, at the confluence that makes the town a river port. Above Kembe the Buyu is not a cargo waterway; below Kembe the Luvasi flows through the basin floor, where it is used for irrigation and smallholder farming. The river is the reason Kembe exists as a junction — rail, road and river meet at the confluence.

Seasonal variation [edit]

The Luvasi does not flood in the same way the Buyu does. The Buyu valley floods in the second week of February each year, and the weir at Mbuyane keeps the upper channel full for irrigation while the distributary at the drift stays low. The Luvasi's seasonal variation is driven by the Maweru Massif's rainfall: the river is highest in January and February, when the rains on the crest feed the basin, and lowest in September and October.

Navigation [edit]

The Luvasi Pan, where the river ends, is an endorheic salt pan in the western basin. It has no outflow and no connection to any sea or ocean. The water that enters the pan is lost to evaporation, which is why the basin floor is salt flat and why the river's lower reach is not navigable for bulk cargo.
