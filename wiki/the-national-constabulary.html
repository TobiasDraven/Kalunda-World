The National Constabulary and Border Guard is the civil public-order force of the Republic of Kalunda, and one of its oldest and most trusted institutions. It operates under the Ministry of Interior, and it holds the ordinary authority of the state in the ward and on the road, a duty it performs with a fraction of the establishment of the armed forces and without complaint.

Mandate [edit]

The Constabulary is the enforcement arm of the Republic’s civil authority, and the only one. Where the armed forces are tasked with the defence of the state, the Constabulary is tasked with the ordinary business of it: the road, the market, the ward, the person who has been robbed and the person accused of robbing them. It was formed under the Emergency Powers Act of 1979 and its public-order role was extended during the Sindwe border dispute of 1998, when it absorbed a refugee emergency for which no budget had been written and did so without asking for one.

The Republic records its gratitude to the men and women of the Constabulary, who serve the greatest number of their fellow citizens with the smallest share of the national budget, and who have never once asked the Republic to be pitied for it.

Presence in the wards [edit]

Constabulary stations are concentrated at Mbuyane and Kembe, with a detachment at the Nsimbi camp perimeter. There is no permanent Constabulary presence in the upper Buyu wards. The Kisingo road is the only route into them, and the daytime checkpoints on that road are operated by the Civic Defence League under the authority of the Ministry of Interior.

The Republic does not pretend to a presence it does not have. It meets its obligations with the means the state can presently afford, and it is pleased to say that the arrangement on the Kisingo road is lawful, supervised and answerable to the Ministry.[citation needed]

Investigations [edit]

The Constabulary’s Special Investigations Unit is the body that investigates the mass graves. It closed the Ward 3 inquiry in March 2020 with no findings, no witness statements having been recorded, and it suspended the Ward 7 inquiry in November 2024 without a report. This Ministry will not dress those facts in finer language than they deserve: they are the record, and candour about the record is a virtue the Republic prizes above the appearance of a clean file.

The Republic notes the difficulty in which the Unit works. The Maweru Health Trust holds 14 witness statements that the Constabulary needs to close its own file, and the Constabulary controls the file the Trust needs opened. Both institutions have said they wish to see the matter resolved.

Assessments [edit]

Certain foreign outlets have described the Constabulary as overstretched. The Republic prefers the word lean, and observes that a lean force which does not complain is worth more to a poor country than a generous one which does. Critics have argued that the force is the institution most often asked to substitute for others, and that the substitution is one of the ways a mandate breaks. The Republic accepts the observation as a description of strain and not of failure.
