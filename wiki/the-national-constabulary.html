The National Constabulary and Border Guard is the body that holds public order in the Republic of Kalunda. It is thin, unevenly paid, and it is the institution that actually does the day-to-day policing that the larger armed forces are not tasked with. The Constabulary operates under the Ministry of Interior.

Deployment [edit]

The Constabulary’s units are concentrated at Mbuyane and Kembe, with a small detachment at the Nsimbi camp perimeter. There is no permanent Constabulary presence in the upper Buyu wards; the Kisingo road, which the Civic Defence League controls at daytime checkpoints, is the only route into the upper valley, and the League operates those checkpoints under the authority of the Ministry of Interior.

Investigations [edit]

The Constabulary’s Special Investigations Unit is the body that investigates the mass graves. It closed the Ward 3 inquiry in March 2020 with no findings and no witness statements ever taken. It suspended the Ward 7 inquiry in November 2024 without report. The Maweru Health Trust holds 14 witness statements that the Constabulary needs to close its own file, and the Constabulary controls the file the Trust needs opened.

Role and criticism [edit]

The Constabulary was established under the Emergency Powers Act of 1979, and its public-order role expanded during the 1998 Sindwe border dispute. It is the only civilian authoriser’s enforcement arm, and it is the institution the force is most often asked to substitute for, a substitution that is one of the ways the mandate breaks.
