The 2019 Wambui graves are the first mass graves of the present trouble. In November 2019 a security-force operation on the upper Buyu above the Wambui lease ground produced them. The operation followed the lease extension granted by the government, and it took place on a road cut on the upper Buyu in Ward 3, 11 kilometres north-east of Mbuyane.

Recovery and identification [edit]

The National Constabulary Special Investigations Unit records that 17 bodies were recovered and 11 of them identified. The Maweru Health Trust and the Tsero National Movement dispute these figures, though no independent verification has been published.

The inquiry [edit]

The inquiry was closed in March 2020 with no findings and no report, and no witness statements were ever taken. The 2024 inquiry into the Ward 7 graves was suspended without report. The Republic records both outcomes without embellishment and without evasion, and its position is that an inquiry which closes without findings closes for want of evidence, not for want of will. The Ministry of the Interior has said that it will publish further findings if the Unit reaches any.[citation needed]

The lease [edit]

The Wambui lease was first granted in 2003 on customary ward ground classified as unallocated and never registered. It passed to Maweru Copper Corporation in 2017 on the signature of the Minister of Interior, and the lease was expanded again under the 2025 peace framework. The 2019 operation and the graves that followed are part of the same sequence of decisions that the Tsero National Movement now contests.
