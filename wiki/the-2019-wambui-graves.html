The 2019 Wambui graves

Home | Government | The Country | News | KalundaWiki | Geography | Radio | Travel

Discussion: Talk page

In November 2019 a security-force operation on the upper Buyu above the Wambui lease ground produced the first mass graves of the present trouble. The operation followed the lease extension granted by the government, and it took place on a road cut on the upper Buyu in Ward 3, 11 kilometres north-east of Mbuyane.

The National Constabulary Special Investigations Unit records that 17 bodies were recovered and 11 of them identified. The Maweru Health Trust and the Tsero National Movement dispute these figures, though no independent verification has been published. The inquiry was closed in March 2020 with no findings and no report, and no witness statements were ever taken — which is why no body holds any.

The 2019 inquiry closed without findings. The 2024 inquiry into the Ward 7 graves was suspended without report. The pattern is the point: the 2019 inquiry closed without findings, the 2024 inquiry was suspended without report, and no one has been held responsible for either.

The Wambui lease was first granted in 2003 on customary ward ground classified as unallocated and never registered. It passed to Maweru Copper Corporation in 2017 on the signature of the Minister of Interior, and the lease was expanded again under the 2025 peace framework. The 2019 operation and the graves that followed are part of the same sequence of decisions that the Tsero National Movement now contests.
