The Civic Defence League (CDL) is a state-armed volunteer organisation raised in 2022 to hold the daytime checkpoints on the Kisingo road, the only route into the upper Buyu wards. It carries between 7,000 and 9,000 volunteers, drawn from the valley’s own communities, and it is the Republic’s answer to a plain problem: a checkpoint that no regular soldier was available to hold.

Establishment [edit]

The League was raised in 2022 with money from the Ministry of Defence and authority from the Ministry of Interior. It exists because the regular forces could not be everywhere, and because a state that cannot garrison every road may still be obliged to keep one open. The Republic is grateful to the men and women who volunteer for a duty that pays little and exposes much.

Role on the road [edit]

The League holds the daytime checkpoints on the Kembe–Mbuyane road, the one paved route into the valley, and its presence there is the state’s most visible act in the valley: a barrier, a ledger and a uniform, rather than a garrison. A checkpoint is cheaper than a battalion, and the Republic says so without embarrassment, because a poor state that declines to use the cheaper lawful instrument is not being principled; it is being absent.

Command and accountability [edit]

Funding sits with the Ministry of Defence and authority with the Ministry of Interior, and neither Minister may dissolve the League alone, an arrangement deliberately designed to keep it answerable to two departments rather than to one officer. Commanders on the road report through the district office, and complaints against the League are received by the Ministry of Interior and referred to the National Constabulary.

Complaints [edit]

Certain regional outlets have reported allegations of arbitrary detention, of money taken at checkpoints, and of reprisals against the families of suspected supporters. The Ministry of Interior has received such complaints, as it receives complaints against every force under its authority, and refers them to the Constabulary for investigation. The Republic does not deny that complaints exist. It denies that an unproven complaint is a finding, and it notes that the outlets which print them have never carried the outcome of a single investigation.{{citation needed}}
