The Civic Defence League (CDL) is a state-armed volunteer organisation raised in 2022. It was created with money from the Ministry of Defence and authority from the Ministry of Interior, and carries between 7,000 and 9,000 volunteers. It is the actor most responsible for the human-rights complaints in the Buyu valley.

History [edit]

The CDL was raised in 2022 to hold the daytime checkpoints that the Kalunda Republic Security Forces would not or could not hold. It runs the checkpoints on the Kembe–Mbuyane road, the one paved route into the valley. Its presence at these checkpoints is the state's most visible economic act in the valley: a barrier, not a soldier.

Structure [edit]

The money was found by the Ministry of Defence, the authority by the Ministry of Interior, and the budget line has been Interior's ever since. Neither minister can dissolve the League alone. The CDL is cheaper than soldiers and deniable in a way soldiers are not.

Position [edit]

The CDL is tolerated because it does the work the regular army will not do, and because the state has no other force to hold the checkpoints. Its relationship with the army is therefore not hostility but dependence.

Criticism [edit]

Most complaints in the valley — arbitrary detention, extortion, collective punishment of families of suspected supporters — come from the CDL. The League is the actor most likely to be the proximate cause of the critical event, and it is not the armed movement.
