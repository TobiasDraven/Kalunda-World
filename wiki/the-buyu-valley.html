The Buyu valley is a valley in the south of the Republic of Kalunda. It runs north to south along the western foot of the Tsero Escarpment and measures approximately 140 km by 40 km, with a floor between 400 and 700 metres above sea level. It is home to about 410,000 people and is divided into nine wards. The principal town is Mbuyane.

The valley is the country's breadbasket and its flood country. Its ground and its population are the subject of the Wambui lease, and it has been the principal area of operations of the security forces since the emergency of 2024.

Geography [edit]

The valley is bounded to the north by the Maweru massif, whose highest point is Mount Wambui at 2,410 metres, the highest ground in the state and visible from most of the valley. To the east the Tsero Escarpment forms a wall along the whole length of the valley. Country relief runs west to east from the Kalunda Basin at 350 to 500 metres, across the central plateau at 1,100 to 1,500 metres, down to the valley floor, and up to the escarpment crest.

The Buyu river rises below the Kisingo Pass, runs south past Mbuyane and joins the Luvasi at Kembe. The valley floor is flat. The river takes the low ground in the second week of February; in January the flood plain is soft and the north road is already degrading.

Settlement [edit]

Most of the population lives on the valley floor and in the lower wards. The upper wards are highland cultivation country. Principal settlements include:

Economy [edit]

The valley grows maize as its breadbasket crop, together with groundnuts, citrus and vegetables. Its customary ward ground is the subject of the copper concession granted under the Wambui lease, which the government states accounts for 54 per cent of national export earnings.

The 2024 emergency [edit]

See also: The 2024 emergency and The 2019 Wambui graves.

The valley has been under emergency measures since 2024. Displacement into the camps on the flood plain, including Nsimbi, followed operations in the upper wards. The events of 12 and 14 January 2026 are reported in the national and regional press; see News.

Communications [edit]

The Kisingo Pass, 45 km north-east of Mbuyane, is the only vehicle crossing of the escarpment for 60 km in either direction and controls the upper road net. The Mbuyane to Nsimbi spur crosses a distributary of the Buyu at Mbiza drift, 8 km from the depot, where light vehicles and four-wheel drives may cross and a heavy truck may not. A bypass exists across soft flood-plain ground and will not take a convoy twice.
