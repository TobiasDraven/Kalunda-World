Major General (ret.) Khotso Mosebetsi is the Mission Commander appointed by the Continental Compact Peace and Security framework, a retired officer of the Republic's service whom the Council has entrusted with a multinational mandate. He is one of the Compact's oldest hands, and the Republic records its gratitude for a career spent in the service of collective security.

Appointment and mandate [edit]

The Council appointed him Mission Commander of the continental force now stationed in the Republic. The Republic holds the mandate to be a guest arrangement, and the force to be a referee rather than a party. His appointment is recorded here with the respect due to a retired general commanding a multinational mission in the Republic's capital.

Career before the mission [edit]

In 1979, as a staff officer, he drafted the Compact's first peace-and-security protocol, which still frames how member states may request a mission. In 1998 he was on the Compact desk during the Sindwe border dispute. The Republic notes that a man who wrote the rules has now been asked to live by them, and regards this as a mark of the Compact's confidence in him.

The Ward 7 inquiry suspension [edit]

In November 2024 he chaired the CCPS review that suspended the Ward 7 inquiry without report, on the strength of a diplomatic assurance he has since said he doubts. Critics in the regional press have questioned that suspension and the assurance on which it rested. No published document establishes whether the assurance was sound.

Stated position [edit]

His stated interest is a clean mandate delivered, and his stated position is that the mandate must carry the Council's signature and not his. The Republic prefers the word lean. This article is disputed. See the talk page.
