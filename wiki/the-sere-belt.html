The Sere belt

Discussion: Talk page

The Sere belt is a mineral-bearing formation that runs east to west beneath the Kalunda Sands along the northern border of the Republic of Kalunda. It is the country's only mineral resource of consequence and the source of copper and cobalt, which together account for 54 percent of Kalunda's export earnings.

The mines are concentrated in the northern border region, close to the Sindwe frontier. The workforce is drawn from the basin communities and from the northern wards, though the upper Buyu wards — the Tsero heartland — were excluded from the mine payroll after the 2019 lease extension. The state takes revenue through the finance portfolio, which is held by the President before she was President; the copper royalty stream, corporate tax and equity dividend are the largest hard-currency lines in the fiscus.

The railway from Lukunda to Kembe, 190 kilometres of the country's single 1,050-kilometre line, was built primarily to serve the Sere belt. Concentrate trains run south to Nsundu, and the return traffic is fuel, machinery and, in a bad year, maize. The Sere belt's output is therefore the reason the rail corridor exists and the reason the country has no alternate heavy-cargo route.

The 2025 peace framework expanded the Wambui lease into the upper Buyu ground, and the Tsero National Movement has contested the expansion ever since. The lease's revenue line is 54 percent of export earnings, and the state is not prepared to let that line be reopened by a settlement that reverses the lease.
