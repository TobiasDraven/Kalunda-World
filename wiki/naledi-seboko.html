Naledi Seboko is the President of the Republic of Kalunda. She came to the Presidency from the finance ministry, and her administration rests on copper revenue and on the Wambui lease. Her term has been dominated by the emergency in the Buyu valley, the arrival of the continental force, and persistent demands that she apologise for the land classification of 2003.

What is undisputed [edit]

1954: the Wambui concession to Meridian Copper Works set the pattern she inherited — customary ground leased for copper, no ward ever consulted. 2003: the Wambui lease was first granted on customary ward ground classified unallocated and never registered, on her watch as finance minister. 2019: the lease extension and the security-force operation that produced the first mass graves in Ward 3, November 2019 — 17 recovered, 11 identified; the inquiry closed in March 2020 with no findings and no witness statements ever taken. Copper lease revenue accounts for 54 per cent of export earnings. She has set the February flood peak as the deadline for a quiet valley.

What the state says [edit]

The government maintains that the 2003 classification and the 2019 extension were lawful, that the Wambui lease underwrites the budget her stability platform rests on, and that the mandate for the continental force is a guest arrangement rather than an occupation.

What is said against it [edit]

Critics, in the valley and in the regional press, hold that the President signed off on the November 2019 security-force operation, and that the 2003 classification of customary ward ground as unallocated was unlawful. The regional press has pressed for an apology for 2003; the President has refused.

What the record cannot settle [edit]

Whether the 2003 classification was lawful, and whether the President signed off on the November 2019 operation. No available document settles either question.
