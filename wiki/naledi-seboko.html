Naledi Seboko is the President of the Republic of Kalunda. She came to the office from the finance ministry, where the Wambui lease was first granted in 2003, and her administration rests on copper revenue and on that lease. Her term has been dominated by the emergency in the Buyu valley, the arrival of the continental force, and a demand for an apology she has refused to give.

Career in the finance ministry [edit]

Seboko came to the Presidency from the finance ministry. The Wambui lease was first granted in 2003, during her tenure there, on customary ward ground that had been classified unallocated and never registered; the classification is the instrument on which the lease rests, and it is the decision her critics return to. {{citation needed}}

Copper lease revenue now accounts for 54 per cent of the Republic’s export earnings. Officials who have worked with her describe a minister who read the ledger rather than the brief, a reputation she carried into the Presidency and has not spent.

Presidency [edit]

The Presidency rests on two commitments: that the lease revenue is spent on stability rather than on patronage, and that the continental force is a guest arrangement and not an occupation. The Republic maintains both. Her administration has set the February flood peak as the deadline for a quiet valley, a timetable that officials repeat more often than they explain.

The Wambui lease and the 2019 graves [edit]

In 2019 the lease was extended, and in November of that year a security-force operation in Ward 3 produced the first mass graves: 17 bodies were recovered and 11 identified. The subsequent inquiry closed in March 2020 with no findings and no witness statements taken. The government holds that both the 2003 classification and the 2019 extension were lawful, and that the ground was lawfully available.{{citation needed}}

Critics in the valley and in the regional press contend that the classification of customary ward ground as unallocated was unlawful, and that the President authorised the November 2019 operation. No published document establishes either claim, and no published document refutes them.

Criticism and public standing [edit]

The regional press has pressed for an apology for 2003 in every year since the graves were found. The President has declined, on the ground that an apology would concede the unlawfulness of a decision she holds to have been correct. In the upper wards she is spoken of less as a President than as the woman who signed, which her office regards as a misunderstanding of where a signature ends and an administration begins.

Among donors and creditors she is regarded as the reason the Republic still services its debt. The Ministry of Information records that the Republic is grateful for her service, and notes that gratitude is not the same as agreement.
