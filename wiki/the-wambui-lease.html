The Wambui lease is a mineral concession over the Sere belt in the upper Buyu valley, granted in 2003 over roughly 6,200 hectares, transferred to Maweru Copper Corporation in 2017, extended by ministerial notice in 2019 and expanded under the 2025 peace framework. It is the single most consequential legal instrument in the valley, and the Republic is content to let the record of it be read.

The grant of 2003 [edit]

The lease was first granted in 2003, as a thirty-year cultivation-and-prospecting lease over roughly 6,200 hectares of the Sere belt. The ground was classified unallocated under the Concessions and Leasing Ordinance of 1948, a classification made by the Ministry of the Interior and recorded in the register; the Republic’s position is that the classification was correct and that the ground was lawfully available.[citation needed]

It is the instrument on which everything since rests, and it is the decision the valley returns to. A concession granted over ground that a ward has farmed for a century is not a small act, and the Republic has never pretended that it was.

Holders [edit]

Meridian Copper Works held the lease from 2003. In 2017 it passed to Maweru Copper Corporation, a state-linked company, on the signature of the Minister of the Interior and recorded as an assignment with ministerial consent. The two companies are separate legal entities, and the Republic has been asked why the transfer was necessary and has answered that a concession of national importance belongs with a company the state can hold to account.[citation needed]

The 2019 extension [edit]

In 2019 the lease was extended by ministerial notice, and in November of that year a security-force operation on the ground above it produced the first mass graves in Ward 3. The Republic records both events in the same paragraph because they happened in the same year and because the valley does not separate them; it declines to accept that the second was caused by the first. See The 2019 Wambui graves.

The 2025 expansion [edit]

The peace framework of 2025 expanded the lease into the upper Buyu ground, and the Tsero National Movement has contested the expansion ever since. The Republic’s position is that the expansion is lawful, that it was agreed with the parties the framework could reach, and that a revenue line carrying 54 per cent of export earnings cannot be surrendered to satisfy a claim which no court has upheld.[citation needed]
