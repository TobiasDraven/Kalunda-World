The Compact Council on Peace and Security (CCPS) is the operational council of the Southern African Compact, distinct from the Continental Assembly. Where the Assembly determines, the Council deploys: it authorises missions, endorses their commanders, and holds the pen on their scope through a troika of three member states. The Republic is grateful for the Council’s endorsement of the mission to Kalunda, and for the care with which it has kept that mission inside the mandate it was given.

The troika [edit]

The troika is the mechanism by which three member states hold the pen on any deployment. Each deployment requires its endorsement, and its authority is how the Council keeps a mission’s scope from being widened or narrowed by any single state. The Council endorsed the mission to the Republic of Kalunda on 13 January 2026 and appointed Brigadier-General (acting) Lefu Thabane as Force Commander.

Relation to the Assembly [edit]

The Council is the Assembly’s operational arm. The Assembly determines by member-state delegation and issues determinations that bind signatories; the Council manages the deployment, the commander’s appointment and the oversight of a mission in the field. The Republic has reason to be glad of the division: a determination can be argued about for a year, whereas a deployment must be supervised every morning.

Interest in scope [edit]

The Council has no interest in widening the mission to include the lease or the corridor, because widening it would require the troika to take a position on a member state’s internal land question, and that position would break the consensus that authorises the mission. The Republic understands this, has said so plainly to its partners, and does not mistake it for indifference.
