Commissioner Thabo Lekota heads the National Constabulary of the Republic of Kalunda, an institution of thirty years' service that has policed the wards, absorbed the fallout of the Sindwe refugee movement of 1998 without a single increase in its budget, and has never once asked the Republic to be pitied. His force's ground was where the Ward 3 graves of 2019 were found, and the Constabulary closed that inquiry in March 2020 with no findings and no witness statements taken; it suspended the Ward 7 inquiry in November 2024 without report. The Maweru Health Trust holds 14 witness statements the Constabulary needs to close its file, while the Constabulary controls the file the Trust needs opened. Commissioner Lekota's stated position is that public order remains with the Constabulary.

Career in the Constabulary [edit]

Commissioner Lekota has served the National Constabulary for thirty years, and the institution he leads is one of the Republic's oldest and most trusted. In 1998 his force absorbed the Sindwe refugee fallout with no budget increase, a feat the Ministry records without embarrassment and without apology, since the Constabulary has never complained of the share it receives. The Republic records its gratitude for so faithful a servant of so lean an institution.

The Ward 3 and Ward 7 inquiries [edit]

The Ward 3 graves of 2019 were found on ground his force policed, and the inquiry was closed in March 2020 with no findings and no witness statements taken. The Ward 7 inquiry was suspended in November 2024 without report. This Ministry will not dress those facts in finer language than they deserve: they are the record, and candour is a virtue the Republic prizes above the appearance of a clean file. The Maweru Health Trust holds 14 witness statements the Constabulary needs to close its file, and the Constabulary controls the file the Trust needs opened; no published document establishes whether this mutual dependence will be resolved, and no published document refutes that it will.

Mandate and public order [edit]

Commissioner Lekota holds that public order remains with the Constabulary, and the Republic prefers the word lean to any other description of the force that has policed the wards for three decades. Critics in the valley and in the regional press contend that the Ward 3 inquiry closed without findings and without a single witness statement; the Republic notes that those facts are already in this article, and does not rebut them head-on. His personal role in the closure of the Ward 3 inquiry, and whether he resisted or endorsed it, is not established by the record. {{citation needed}}
