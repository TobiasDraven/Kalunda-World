Discussion — The Wambui lease

Talk page

14 Jan 2026 09:00 LeaseWatch (talk | contribs): The article says the lease was granted in 1954 to Meridian Copper Works. This is wrong. The lease was first granted in 2003 as a thirty-year cultivation-and-prospecting lease over roughly 6,200 hectares. Meridian Copper Works was the original grantee of the 1954 concession, which is a different instrument. The article conflates two separate leases and needs to be corrected.

14 Jan 2026 09:30 102.89.33.7 (talk | contribs): @LeaseWatch The 1954 date in the article refers to the original Wambui concession under the Concessions and Leasing Ordinance of 1948. The 2003 lease is a separate instrument. The article is unclear about which lease it is describing. Please clarify which lease the article covers.

14 Jan 2026 10:15 LeaseWatch (talk | contribs): The article needs to distinguish between the 1954 concession and the 2003 lease. The 2003 lease was granted by ministerial notice as state land classified unallocated. The 1954 concession was granted under the 1948 Ordinance. These are different legal instruments with different terms and different holders.

14 Jan 2026 11:00 102.89.33.7 (talk | contribs): The lease terms are also disputed. The 2003 lease was for thirty years, meaning it expires in 2033. But the article says the lease was extended in 2019. The extension was granted by ministerial notice with no consent from the customary holders. The terms of the extension — area, working rights, term — are not stated in the article and should be cited.

14 Jan 2026 11:45 MaweruCorp (talk | contribs): The Maweru Copper Corporation is a state-linked company and the current leaseholder. The 2017 transfer from Meridian Copper Works was recorded as an assignment with ministerial consent. The company's payments to the state are reported annually in the Ministry of Finance budget. The lease payments are not a matter for this article.

14 Jan 2026 12:30 LeaseWatch (talk | contribs): @MaweruCorp The lease payments are exactly the matter for this article. The Wambui lease is a mineral concession over the Sere belt. The Sere belt copper and cobalt account for 54 percent of Kalunda's export earnings. The lease payments are therefore a matter of public record and a matter for this article. If the company's payments are reported in the budget, cite the budget line.

14 Jan 2026 13:15 102.89.33.7 (talk | contribs): The lease was extended in 2019 by ministerial notice. The extension enlarged the area, granted working rights, and extended the term to 2048. The customary holders — the highland cultivation families in the upper wards — were not consulted. The 2011 cadastral survey had marked their terraces, and the map was extended over them. This is the dispute the article should be recording.

14 Jan 2026 14:00 MaweruCorp (talk | contribs): The lease terms are a matter of public record. The 2019 extension is recorded in the Ministry of Finance budget. The company's payments are reported annually. The article should cite the budget line and the ministerial notice, not speculate about the terms.

14 Jan 2026 14:45 LeaseWatch (talk | contribs): The article does not cite a single source for the lease terms. The budget line is not cited. The ministerial notice is not cited. The 2003 grant is not cited. The article is a stub with no sources and no verification of the claims it makes. The lease terms should be cited or the article should carry a needs expansion tag.

14 Jan 2026 15:30 MaweruCorp (talk | contribs): The company will not be commenting further on this talk page. The lease is a matter of public record and the budget is available for inspection. The article should be updated to cite the budget line and the ministerial notice.
