The Tsero National Movement is a land-rights campaign founded in 2011 on behalf of highland cultivation communities in the upper Buyu valley. Its political wing is led by Mamello Tsero; its armed wing is the Kalunda National Liberation Front. The Republic acknowledges that the ground the Movement claims is ground the state has never finally settled. It does not follow that the Movement may settle it with a rifle.

Origins [edit]

The Movement formed in 2011, the year the Ralebo cadastral survey clearance redrew the uplands without consulting the ward. Its public voice was a highland teacher who became its head. Its claim is to ground whose title was split from customary tenure at the Plateau Settlement of 1967 and 1968 and never regularised. The Republic has not disputed the history; it disputes the remedy.

Aims [edit]

The Movement’s stated aims are land tenure security and restitution for displaced cultivation families. It campaigns for the reversal of the Wambui lease and for the reopening of the mass-graves inquiry, a demand the Republic understands and has not granted.

Political and armed wings [edit]

The political wing denies that the Movement fired on the Nsimbi convoy of 12 January 2026, and insists that nothing may be negotiated without the upper-ward elders. The Republic notes that the denial is not shared by the Movement’s own military spokesmen, and that a denial issued by one wing of an organisation about the conduct of the other is a claim and not an alibi. [citation needed]

Status [edit]

The Movement is not a registered party, and the state does not negotiate with it. Officers of the Republic have said privately that a settlement will eventually require speaking to somebody, and that the difficulty is not finding somebody to speak to but finding somebody who can stop the mortars.
