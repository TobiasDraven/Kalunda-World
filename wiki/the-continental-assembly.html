The Continental Assembly

Home | Government | The Country | News | KalundaWiki | Geography | Radio | Travel

The Continental Assembly is the collective-security authority of the Southern African Compact (SAC). It is the body that authorises deployments, determines mission mandates, and issues determinations that bind member states. The Assembly meets in Lukunda, the capital of the Republic of Kalunda, and its determinations are recorded as binding on all signatories to the SAC charter.

The Assembly votes by member-state delegation. Each member state holds one vote, and determinations are reached by a two-thirds majority of the sitting delegations. Once a determination is passed, member states are bound to comply; non-compliance is recorded as a breach of the SAC charter and may trigger sanctions or suspension of membership.

The Assembly has authorised two named interventions in the past two decades:

  1. The Sindwe Border Crisis (1998). In 1998, 400,000 Kalundan refugees crossed the border into Sindwe. The Assembly authorised a humanitarian corridor and a border stabilisation mission that ran from January to November 1998. The crossing remains closed more often than open.
  2. The Kalunda Mission (2026). On 13 January 2026, the Assembly determined the mission authorisation for the Republic of Kalunda. The determination was routed through the Southern African Compact to the continental security council, and the mission began on 14 January 2026.
