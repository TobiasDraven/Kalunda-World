The Continental Assembly is the collective-security authority of the Southern African Compact. It authorises deployments, determines mandates, and issues determinations that bind its signatories. It meets in Lukunda, the capital of the Republic, and the Republic is proud to host it: that the body which keeps the peace of the region should sit in a Kalundan chamber is a distinction no other member state has been granted.

Procedure [edit]

The Assembly votes by member-state delegation, one vote each, and reaches determinations by a two-thirds majority of the delegations sitting. Once a determination passes, members are bound to comply; non-compliance is recorded as a breach of the charter and may attract sanctions or suspension. The Republic has never been recorded in breach, a fact its delegation mentions at every appropriate opportunity.

Authorised interventions [edit]

Two have been authorised in two decades. In the Sindwe border crisis of 1998, 400,000 Kalundan refugees crossed the border and the Assembly authorised a humanitarian corridor and a border stabilisation mission running from January to November; the crossing remains closed more often than open. In the Kalunda mission of 2026, the Assembly determined the mission authorisation on 13 January, routed it through the Compact to the continental security council, and the mission began on 14 January.

Relations [edit]

The Assembly determines; the Compact Council deploys. Its security committee is served by Dr Sello Mokoena, and its determinations on the Kalunda graves were adopted in 2019.
