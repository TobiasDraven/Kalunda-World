The Kalunda National Liberation Front (KNLF) is the armed wing of the Tsero National Movement, formed in 2019 after the extension of the Wambui lease and the first mass graves at the road cut in Ward 3. It styles itself a liberation front; the Republic does not use the word. The Republic’s position is that its claims are grievances dressed as a war, and that whatever justice there was in the grievance, it was forfeited the first time the movement put a mortar on a road used by farmers.

Formation [edit]

The Front formed in 2019, in the year of the lease extension and of the security-force operation that produced the first mass graves in Ward 3. Its founding document replaced the Ralebo Memorandum of September 2019, which the Republic had been willing to discuss. See The 2019 Wambui graves.

Composition and strength [edit]

The movement claims 4,000 to 6,000 fighters in the upper Buyu and on the escarpment. The Republic does not accept the figure, and notes that a movement which counts its coerced among its volunteers will always count high. On any figure, a fraction of its strength is disciplined, and the remainder is made up of recruited villagers, deserters and men who were given a rifle and no choice. {{citation needed}}

The Kisingo corridor [edit]

What the Front holds is not a place but a capability: the Kisingo Pass corridor and the upper road net. The Kisingo road is degraded, slow and passable to light vehicles only; heavy vehicles cannot use it. This lets the movement put people, food, arms and information into the valley faster than the state can take them out, and leave faster than it can be followed. It is the one advantage the Republic concedes it.

Stated aims [edit]

The movement’s stated aims are land tenure security and restitution for displaced cultivation families. Its political wing is more careful than its military wing, and the Republic has noted the gap without being persuaded by it: a careful spokesman has never once condemned a mortar fired by his own side.
