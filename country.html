Geography and terrain

Kalunda sits between 350 and 2,410 metres. The western half is the Kalunda Basin: semi-arid thornveld, cattle country, and the salt flats of the Luvasi Pan where the Luvasi river ends. The eastern third is the Tsero Escarpment and the Maweru Massif, the highest ground in the country at 2,410 metres on Mount Wambui, cool and wet on the crest and intensively farmed on the eastern slopes in coffee and tea. Between them runs the Luvasi river, rising on the Maweru Massif and running north-west to Lukunda, then south-east to Kembe, then south-west across the basin for 900 kilometres to the Pan. The crowded, fertile Buyu valley runs 140 kilometres along the western foot of the escarpment, 410,000 people across nine wards, and is the country's breadbasket. Along the northern border the Kalunda Sands rise into dry sand and koppie, and the Sere copper belt runs east to west beneath them. There is no other mineral of consequence. The railway, 1,050 kilometres, runs from Lukunda south-east through Kembe and out through the Lutana corridor to the port of Nsundu, the only way heavy cargo enters or leaves Kalunda.

Climate

Two seasons. The rains run from November to March: 900 to 1,400 millimetres on the escarpment, 350 to 500 in the basin, and the Buyu valley floods in the second week of February. The dry season runs May to September, mild in the basin at night, cold on the central plateau in June and July, hot enough to stop movement by noon in October.

People

Kalunda is five large communities. Kalunda (58%) hold the basin, the rail corridor and the capital. Tsero (14%) are the highland farmers of the escarpment and the majority in the upper wards of the Buyu valley. Kavu (11%) are the riverine fishing communities of the Luvasi. Sera (9%) are the northern pastoralists of the sands. The remainder is mixed and urban, including the long-established Kalunda-Mara mercantile families of the capital.

Economy

Copper and cobalt from the Sere belt are 54% of export earnings. Then tobacco from the escarpment, citrus and groundnuts from the Buyu, river fish. The country imports fuel, machinery and, in a bad year, maize. There are 2,900 kilometres of all-weather road and one railway. GDP is USD 27.8 billion; per capita USD 1,130; inflation 14%; youth unemployment 28%.

To the north, Sindwe: large, mineral-rich, with a border the two states have disputed since 1998. To the west, Kavula: small, landlocked, and on its fourth government this decade, through which most of Kalunda's informal trade moves. To the south, Lutana, coastal, holder of Nsundu, through which Kalunda trades. To the east, Bweni: highland, poor, and the source of the arms that cross the Maweru crest.
