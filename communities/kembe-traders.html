Kembe Traders and Transporters Association

Home | Government | The Country | News | KalundaWiki | Geography | Radio | Travel

The Kembe Traders and Transporters Association represents 34 licensed transporters and 12 trading houses in the river port town of Kembe. The association handles the movement of goods between the rail head at Kembe and the inland communities of the Buyu valley and the Mbuyane corridor.

What It Moves

The association moves approximately 120 tonnes of goods a month. The bulk is fuel (diesel and petrol), maize meal, and construction timber. Other commodities include cement, fertiliser, salted fish from the Luvasi, and copper sheeting from the Sere belt. All freight moves by road from Kembe to Mbuyane (95 km, paved) and then by hand-cart or light vehicle over the last 12 km of gravel to the Mbiza drift.

What It Charges

Transporters charge 850 kalar per tonne from Kembe to Mbuyane and 2,400 kalar per tonne from Kembe to the upper wards beyond the drift. The association sets these rates by agreement among its members and posts them quarterly. Fuel surcharges apply when the pump price changes. Payment is cash on delivery for most traders; credit is extended only to regulars with a known account at the Kembe cooperative bank.

What It Complains About

The association's three standing complaints are: the Mbiza drift closes for an average of 14 days each January, stranding loaded trucks on the Kembe side and forcing traders to store goods at doubled rates; the gravel spur from Mbuyane has not been graded since March 2025; and the district office requires a transit permit for every load, which takes three working days to process and costs 150 kalar per permit. The association says the permit requirement is a formality that delays perishable goods and should be waived for local traders.

The association office is open 08:00 to 16:00, Monday to Saturday, at the Kembe market square.
