Statement of the Tsero National Movement

On the Wambui lease, the wards, and the inquiry that was suspended.

Kisingo, 13 January 2026.

To the people of the Buyu valley, to the wards of the upper Buyu, and to whoever reads this abroad.

This is not a declaration of war. We have been at war for seven years and nobody needed a declaration. This is an account of what was done to us, written so that it cannot be filed and forgotten the way the last two accounts were filed and forgotten.

In 2003 the Republic granted a thirty-year cultivation and prospecting lease over roughly 6,200 hectares of highland flank above the upper Buyu wards, to a private consortium. The ground was ours. It was Tsero cultivation ground, allocated by the ward heads through the Ward Councils, worked in lineage plots, reckoned in memory and in the elders' reckoning. The grant was made under the State Land Act from ground classified as unallocated. Unallocated meant unregistered. Unregistered meant state land. That is the whole trick, and it is the only trick the Republic has ever needed: a classification that converts silence into ownership, because our tenure was never written down.

The 2003 grant was bad enough. The 2019 extension is the one that made us pick up guns. In 2019 the state granted a copper extension — enlarged area, working rights, term extended to 2048 — to the Maweru Copper Corporation, by ministerial notice, the same way as the first grant: administrative notice, no consent from anyone, because no one else is legally recognised as holding. The same households that the 2011 cadastral survey had already marked on a lease map found their terraces leased a second time, with the map extended over them. That told the upper wards that the classification would never be corrected by petition. We petitioned first. We demonstrated. We expected to be embarrassed, and we were embarrassed, and nothing changed.

In November 2019, after the extension, a security-force operation in the upper Buyu left seventeen bodies at a road cut above the lease ground in Ward 3, eleven kilometres north-east of Mbuyane. The Special Investigations Unit investigated. The inquiry closed in March 2020 with no findings, no report, and no witness statements ever taken. Seventeen dead, and no one asked a single witness what happened.

In August 2024, during the state of emergency, thirty-one bodies were recovered from a disused irrigation works in Ward 7, six kilometres above Mbuyane. Twenty-nine were identified. The Special Investigations Unit investigated again. The inquiry was suspended in November 2024 without report. The Maweru Health Trust holds fourteen witness statements. The emergency tolled every restitution deadline running in Buyu District and suspended Tribunal sittings in the district, in the year the district land registry's files were requisitioned into a basement that the February flood reached. The state paused the only machine that could adjudicate our ground, in the year it destroyed the records that machine required.

Two inquiries. Two null outcomes. Nobody responsible either time. That is not an accident of procedure. That is the procedure.

The Land Restitution Tribunal has sat since 2011. No ward-tenure claim has ever been upheld. The standard of proof is a complete file: instrument of tenure, survey plan, certified registry copy, chain of occupation, proof that the loss was by state act. Our tenure was never registered, so there is no instrument. The surveyor's queue runs eighteen months and costs more than a season's surplus. We do not have the money and we do not have the paper, because our paper was never made. The machine does not say no. It says not yet. It has been saying not yet for twelve years.

We will say what we did. In 2019 we formed the Kalunda National Liberation Front. We took the Kisingo Pass corridor and the upper road net in 2021, because without it we could not move and could not leave, and because the state's answer to our petition was the Civic Defence League at our checkpoints. We have fired on convoys. We have killed. We do not pretend that every one of those deaths was necessary, and we do not pretend that every one of those deaths was ours to take. A checkpoint killed in November 2023 was a man with a family and we have not found a sentence that makes that right. We are not going to find one. We put it here because a statement that hides its own hands is a pamphlet, and we are not writing a pamphlet.

We did not sign the 2025 framework. Nobody in the mountains signed it. The framework expanded the lease after signing. The framework left the Civic Defence League outside its provisions. The framework's first test arrived before its force was on station: on 12 January a relief convoy was fired on at the Nsimbi approach, and we were blamed for it. We did not fire on that convoy. We know that saying so costs us nothing, because no one believes it, and we say it anyway, because it is true, and because the convoy was carrying food and vaccine to people who include our own families.

Here is the line that we ask a reader on the government's side to sit with. The protected area at Nsimbi holds 8,300 displaced people, 1,130 of them from Ward 7. The mandate that now sits on them guards roads and a depot. It names roads, not populations. Your force can read its task as covering the spur, or it can read its task as covering the people, and those two readings are not the same act. One of them is a choice, and the choice is not forced by the mandate. It is forced by you.

Our demands are not complicated. Repeal the 2019 extension. Suspend the lease pending adjudication. Reopen both inquiries with independent investigators and published reports. Fund the Tribunal's Buyu circuit and accept oral evidence as founding evidence, not as explanation. Release the detainees of the emergency sweeps. And let the wards' water rights be heard, because the lease took the upper Buyu's water with it and the wards' irrigation works are now mass graves.

We will talk. We have always talked. We talked in 2011 and we talked in 2021 and we will talk again. But we will not talk while the lease runs and the inquiries stay closed, because that is not a negotiation, that is a request to wait.

Tsero National Movement. Kisingo. 13 January 2026.
